# Matching evaluation

Generated by `scripts/eval-report.mts` from the labeled sets in `tests/eval/`. Regenerate with `npx tsx scripts/eval-report.mts`; `npm test` fails if a metric drops below the floor recorded in `tests/eval.test.ts`.

**Labels are by an AI labeler (Claude (AI) from register lookups and the job postings; needs spot-checking by a human) and have not been independently verified.** Cases marked `unsure`/`unclear` are excluded from the metrics and counted. Samples are small, so rates show a 95% Wilson interval: read the interval, not the point estimate. Drawn 2026-11-01 from the live data; the sets are frozen, so a change in the matcher moves the numbers and a change in the data does not.

Two different questions are measured, and kept apart on purpose:

1. **Sponsor-record matching**: is this employer the same organisation as an entry on the official register (UK Home Office, Dutch IND, Irish permits)?
2. **Vacancy sponsorship**: does this posting itself say it offers or rules out visa sponsorship? An employer on the register can post a role that says "no sponsorship", and the reverse.

## 1. Sponsor-record matching

Population when drawn: 885 employers (8152 active jobs) linked to a register entry, 323 employers (1124 jobs) not linked.

### 1a. Board brand to register entry (`brandMatches`)

A job board's company name is attached to a register entry, and every job on that board inherits the entry. **Positive = the pipeline accepts the pair; correct = the entry is the same organisation.** Precision is the share of accepted links that are the right organisation, and it is what backs the "Licensed sponsor" badge.

| Set | Precision | Recall | TP | FP | FN | TN |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random sample of linked employers (68 pairs) | 99% (67/68, 95% CI 92%–100%) | 100% (67/67, 95% CI 95%–100%) | 67 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| Stress set: brand collisions, enriched on purpose (105 pairs) | 75% (79/105, 95% CI 66%–83%) | 100% (79/79, 95% CI 95%–100%) | 79 | 26 | 0 | 0 |

- The **random sample** is the honest estimate of how often a board link is right across all linked employers: correct: 67/68 = 99% (95% CI 92%–100%).
- The **stress set** is drawn from the 137 boards whose brand is a prefix of several register entries (441 of 882 board links are prefix-only; 137 of those have rivals). Its precision is deliberately pessimistic and is not a population rate. It shows what kind of company the rule lets through.
- Recall is 100% because the rule accepts every prefix; the cost is the false positives below.
- Excluded as unsure: 20.

### 1b. Employer name to register entry (`licenceFor`, name path)

A job's employer name is linked to a register entry when their normalised keys are equal. **Positive = a link was made.** A link to the wrong entry counts against both precision and recall.

- **Employers already linked by name** (10 drawn): 10/10 linked to the correct entry.
- **Employers with no link** (75 labeled, drawn at random): 65 are correctly unlinked (not on the register), 10 are on the register and missed. These were chosen *because* they are unlinked, so the matcher links none of them by construction; the miss rate below is the informative number, not a recall figure.

Of the 75 labeled unlinked employers, 10 are on the register under a name the exact-key rule cannot match (brand versus legal name: "Sumo Logic" vs "Sumologic Limited", "Elastic" vs "elasticsearch B.V."). That is the miss rate among unlinked employers: 10/75 = 13% (95% CI 7%–23%).

**Population estimate (unlinked employers missed by name linking, weighted to the real counts):** about 43 of the 323 unlinked employers (range 24–74) are on the register. Taking the linked employers as correct at the random-sample precision, employer-level recall is roughly **95%** (range 92%–97%). Unlinked employers have fewer jobs each (3.5 vs 9.2), so job-level recall is higher than employer-level.

## 2. Vacancy sponsorship (`jdSponsorship`)

Labels: `yes` = the posting says it sponsors or supports visas, `no` = it says it does not (or that you must already have the right to work without sponsorship), `none` = it says nothing about sponsorship. `unclear` cases (conditional or merely implied wording, e.g. "case by case", plain "must have the right to work") are excluded and counted. Duplicated boilerplate (one employer, many jobs, same sentence) is counted once. The mix is enriched for postings that mention visas and for the hard ones the matcher used to miss, so these are not population rates.

Two sets, because the matcher was changed after the first one was scored:

- **Tuned set** (91 cases): used to find and fix bugs (curly apostrophes, "not currently able to sponsor", negated sentences read as offers). Its scores are optimistic by construction.
- **Held-out set** (51 cases): drawn after the fix, from different postings, labeled before the matcher was run on it, and **never used for tuning**. This is the number to quote. If it is ever used to tune, draw a new one (`scripts/eval-draw-jd.mts --holdout` documents how the draws are ordered).

| Set / class | Precision | Recall | TP | FP | FN | TN |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Held-out, "yes" (offers sponsorship) | 100% (8/8, 95% CI 68%–100%) | 73% (8/11, 95% CI 43%–90%) | 8 | 0 | 3 | 34 |
| Held-out, "no" (rules it out) | 100% (13/13, 95% CI 77%–100%) | 72% (13/18, 95% CI 49%–88%) | 13 | 0 | 5 | 27 |
| Tuned, "yes" (offers sponsorship) | 100% (11/11, 95% CI 74%–100%) | 100% (11/11, 95% CI 74%–100%) | 11 | 0 | 0 | 64 |
| Tuned, "no" (rules it out) | 100% (31/31, 95% CI 89%–100%) | 100% (31/31, 95% CI 89%–100%) | 31 | 0 | 0 | 44 |

Held-out: when a posting states a position, the matcher gets it right: 21/29 = 72% (95% CI 54%–85%). Tuned set: 42/42 = 100% (95% CI 92%–100%). Excluded as unclear: 6 held-out, 16 tuned.

**Before the fix** (same tuned set, matcher as of the previous commit): "yes" precision 63% (5/8), recall 45% (5/11); "no" precision 100%, recall 65% (20/31). The three false "yes" answers were postings saying "We can't offer visa sponsorship" (curly apostrophe) or "We are not currently able to sponsor visas": a student would have been told the role sponsors when it does not.
